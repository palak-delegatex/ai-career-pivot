A career pivot plan should help you decide what to do next and what evidence would change your mind. Start with one role, one important uncertainty, and one action that tests it. A long list of courses is not enough to answer whether the work fits you.

[Download the free career-pivot plan worksheet](/worksheets/career-pivot-plan.md). It is a plain-text Markdown file: open it in a text editor or copy it into your notes. There is no form to complete and no payment required for this worksheet.

If you are still choosing a direction, start with the [guide to pivoting into AI without starting over](/blog/how-to-pivot-into-ai-without-starting-over-2026). This page is for turning a possible direction into a written experiment.

## Copy this plan

### 1. Name the role and the reason

Write: “I want to test **[role]** in **[industry or team type]** because my experience in **[specific work]** may transfer to **[specific responsibility]**.”

Avoid a target as broad as “work in AI.” You need a role description specific enough to compare with your experience. Save the source and date of each job posting you use; a title alone does not explain the work.

### 2. Put your constraints on the page

List the time you can actually use each week, location or working-hours requirements, and any qualifications you cannot bypass. Keep private financial details in your own notes. Your plan should fit your circumstances before you commit money or change employment.

### 3. Map experience to evidence

For each relevant skill, record something you actually did, your contribution, and an artifact or explanation you could share. “Stakeholder management” is a label. “Gathered conflicting requirements and documented the agreed tradeoffs” describes work someone can discuss with you.

If you are unsure how to name your skills, [CareerOneStop's Skills Matcher](https://www.careeronestop.org/Toolkit/Skills/skills-matcher.aspx) offers career suggestions based on self-ratings of workplace skills. Treat suggestions as options to investigate, not a hiring decision.

### 4. Choose a gap and a small test

Use the [skills-gap worksheet](/blog/career-change-skills-gap-worksheet) to distinguish missing experience from experience you have not explained clearly. Choose one gap that matters to the target work. Define a work sample or conversation that would help you learn whether that gap is manageable.

### 5. Set a review date and a decision rule

Write down what you will review, who can give relevant feedback, and what you will do next. Your options can be “continue,” “narrow the role,” or “test another direction.” A useful plan allows you to change your mind.

## Worked example: operations to AI implementation

**Fictional example for illustration. This is not a customer story, a verified job description, or a result produced by our tool.**

An operations coordinator wants to explore implementation work involving AI-assisted processes. Their draft plan might look like this:

- **Role hypothesis:** Implementation coordinator on a team introducing an AI-assisted support workflow.
- **Existing evidence:** Experience documenting handoffs, gathering user feedback, and tracking unresolved issues.
- **Uncertainty:** Whether they can evaluate model-generated drafts and explain when a person must intervene.
- **Small test:** Create synthetic support requests, define what a good response must contain, and review draft responses against that rubric.
- **Artifact:** A short workflow diagram, an evaluation table, and examples of failures requiring human review.
- **Review:** Ask a practitioner whether the artifact reflects the responsibilities in a real posting. Record missing requirements instead of assuming the project proves readiness.
- **Decision rule:** Continue if the work interests them and the feedback identifies a feasible next gap; otherwise revise the target.

The value is the connection between an existing strength and a specific unanswered question. The exercise does not establish that this person meets an employer's requirements.

## Turn the plan into a week you can finish

Choose an output rather than an aspiration. “Learn AI” has no completion condition. “Draft an evaluation rubric and review three synthetic examples” does. The number of examples is your planning choice, not a certification threshold.

At the review date, record what you completed, what surprised you, and the next question. If you need a longer sequence, use the [90-day planning guide](/blog/the-90-day-plan-to-pivot-into-an-ai-role-2026) as a structure to adapt, not a deadline for getting hired.

You can complete this worksheet entirely on your own. The optional free snapshot offers starting suggestions; it is not the paid personalized roadmap and does not guarantee job fit, interviews, or an offer.
