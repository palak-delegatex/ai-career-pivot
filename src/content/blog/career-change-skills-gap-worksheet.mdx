A skills-gap analysis starts with two questions: what does this job require, and what evidence do I have that I can do it? The answer is more useful than a list of matching keywords. It can show you where to practice, where to clarify your experience, and where you need more information.

[Download the free skills-gap worksheet (CSV)](/worksheets/career-change-skills-gap.csv). Open it in a spreadsheet application or import it into Google Sheets. The file contains column headings and blank rows for your own analysis; it does not score you or send your entries to us.

## Start with a specific posting

Choose a posting for a role you would seriously consider. Save its URL, date, and exact wording for the requirements you want to examine. Read the responsibilities as well as the qualifications section.

For broader occupational context, [O*NET's occupation reports](https://www.onetonline.org/help/online/details) describe tasks, work activities, and technology skills. They can help explain a role family, but the employer's actual posting is the source for that specific vacancy.

Separate “required” from “preferred” when the posting does so. Mark unclear language as unclear. Do not let an AI summary silently change a mandatory credential into an optional skill.

## Fill in one row per requirement

Use these fields in the download:

- **Requirement and source:** Copy the wording you are evaluating and where it came from.
- **Required or preferred:** Use the employer's wording, or record that it was not specified.
- **My evidence:** Name the work, your contribution, and what you can truthfully demonstrate.
- **Status:** Choose demonstrated, partly demonstrated, missing, or unclear.
- **Next action:** Identify a work sample, clarification question, or focused practice task.
- **Review date:** Decide when you will revisit the row.

A course completion can be evidence of learning. It does not automatically establish that you can perform the work independently. Likewise, an unfamiliar job title does not mean you lack every underlying skill.

## Worked example: customer support to implementation

**The following requirements and experience are fictional teaching examples, not a current vacancy or a customer outcome.**

### Requirement: document customer workflows

**Evidence:** “I mapped our support escalation process and maintained the handoff notes.”

**Status:** Partly demonstrated. The person has relevant process documentation experience but needs to explain its scope and their contribution.

**Next action:** Prepare an anonymized workflow example and explain what they changed. Do not invent a reduction in handling time if none was measured.

### Requirement: evaluate AI-generated support responses

**Evidence:** “I use a chatbot to draft messages.”

**Status:** Missing evidence of evaluation. Using a tool does not, by itself, demonstrate a repeatable quality-review process.

**Next action:** Build a small synthetic example set, define a review rubric, and document errors and escalation rules. Ask a practitioner whether this resembles the target responsibility.

### Requirement: experience with a named enterprise platform

**Evidence:** Experience with a different support platform.

**Status:** Unclear until the employer's expectations are understood. Similar concepts may transfer, but the platforms are not interchangeable credentials.

**Next action:** Compare the workflows and ask whether equivalent experience is acceptable. Record the answer instead of marking the requirement complete by assumption.

## Choose the next action without creating a misleading score

Do not count completed rows and call the result your chance of being hired. Requirements differ in importance, and employers evaluate context this worksheet cannot see.

Start by identifying explicit eligibility requirements. Then choose a recurring responsibility you can investigate through a concrete piece of work. Keep “I have not done this” separate from “I have done something similar but need to explain it.” They require different next steps.

If an AI tool helps organize your notes, ask it to quote the requirement, identify the supporting evidence you supplied, and mark uncertainty. Review the output against the original documents. Never accept invented experience or performance numbers.
