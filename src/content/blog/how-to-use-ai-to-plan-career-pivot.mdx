Use AI as a drafting partner for your career pivot: give it evidence from your work, ask it to separate facts from guesses, and turn the output into questions you can investigate. A persuasive answer is not proof that a role fits you or that an employer will hire you.

The five prompts below work as a sequence. You can also [download the free career-pivot plan worksheet](/worksheets/career-pivot-plan.md) and complete the same exercise yourself. No purchase is needed to use the prompts or worksheet.

## Before you start: prepare evidence you can safely share

List a few responsibilities, projects, and accomplishments. Include your contribution and any outcomes you can substantiate. Leave out customer details, internal documents, credentials, and confidential employer information. You can describe a workflow using a synthetic example when the real details are sensitive.

Keep a separate list of practical constraints: available time, location, working hours, and qualifications you may need. You do not need to disclose account balances or family details to explain that a plan must fit a limited budget or schedule.

Use the assistant available to you. This guide does not present a benchmark of ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini, and it does not promise a particular completion time. Assess the output by how well it uses your evidence and identifies uncertainty.

## Prompt 1: inventory transferable experience

> I am exploring a career change. Here are responsibilities and projects from my work: [describe them without confidential information].
>
> For each item, identify a potentially transferable skill, quote the evidence I supplied, and explain the connection. Separate demonstrated experience from assumptions. Do not invent metrics, tools, responsibilities, or qualifications. Ask me about missing information rather than filling it in.
>
> Return a short list I can verify, with one question for each uncertain item.

**Review the result:** Replace broad labels with work you actually did. If the assistant calls you a project manager because you attended project meetings, correct the inference. Transferable experience needs an explanation, not a more impressive title.

If you need language for skills you already use, [CareerOneStop's Skills Matcher](https://www.careeronestop.org/Toolkit/Skills/skills-matcher.aspx) provides career suggestions from self-rated workplace skills. It is another exploration aid, not evidence that you meet a particular job's requirements.

## Prompt 2: propose role hypotheses

> Based only on the verified experience above, suggest a small set of role families to investigate. For each, explain which responsibilities might transfer, what you do not know, and what evidence would make the option less suitable.
>
> Do not claim that these roles are hiring, predict salary, or invent current job postings. Give me search terms and questions to use when I review actual vacancies.

**Review the result:** Choose one direction to investigate first. Find postings in the location and work arrangement you need. Save the source and date. Compare responsibilities across employers instead of assuming a fashionable title means the same job everywhere.

For a broader introduction to role selection, see [how to pivot into AI without starting over](/blog/how-to-pivot-into-ai-without-starting-over-2026).

## Prompt 3: separate skill gaps from evidence gaps

> Here is a job posting: [paste the relevant public text]. Here is my verified experience: [paste your edited list].
>
> Quote each requirement and distinguish required from preferred qualifications when the posting does. Map it to evidence I actually supplied. Label it demonstrated, partly demonstrated, missing, or unclear. Explain uncertainty and do not convert the result into a hiring probability or an employer ATS score.
>
> For each gap, suggest a question or small work sample that would help me investigate it.

**Review the result:** Check every requirement against the original posting. A related skill may transfer without being an exact substitute. An assistant cannot waive an employer's mandatory credentials.

Use the [skills-gap worksheet and fictional example](/blog/career-change-skills-gap-worksheet) to record your edits. If you use the free site's job-posting quick-check, remember that it identifies skills in the posting; without your resume it does not establish your personal match.

## Prompt 4: make the plan fit your constraints

> My constraints are: [available time, location or working-hours needs, spending limit for learning, and any non-negotiable requirements].
>
> Review the role hypothesis and gaps above. Flag suggestions that conflict with these constraints. Identify questions I need to resolve myself or with an appropriate professional. Do not invent salary figures, visa eligibility, financial projections, or a deadline for getting hired.
>
> Suggest a smaller experiment I can do within the constraints I gave you.

**Review the result:** Treat the output as a checklist. Verify costs and eligibility with the relevant provider or employer before making a commitment. Keep consequential financial or legal decisions outside a chatbot's unverified assumptions.

## Prompt 5: define a career experiment

> Turn the role hypothesis and highest-priority uncertainty into a small experiment. Include one concrete output, the materials I need, the time I can allocate, a quality-check method, and someone with relevant experience who could give feedback.
>
> End with a review date and decision criteria: continue, narrow the target, or test another direction. Do not describe a practice project as professional experience or guarantee an interview.

**Review the result:** You should be able to explain what you will produce and what you hope to learn. For example, a synthetic support workflow and an evaluation rubric can test whether implementation work interests you. Completing the exercise does not automatically prove job readiness.

Copy the result into the [career-pivot plan template](/blog/career-pivot-plan-template). Keep the plan somewhere you will revisit, and record what changed after feedback.

## What to check before trusting the output

Look for unsupported numbers, invented accomplishments, missing requirements, and recommendations that ignore your constraints. When something sounds unusually confident, ask what evidence supports it. A citation still needs to be opened and checked.

Do not assume that a generated plan includes ongoing coaching, reminders, or automatic progress tracking. Check what your chosen tool actually provides. You can schedule your own review and ask a practitioner to critique the work sample.

The purpose of this sequence is to make your next action clearer. It is reasonable to revise a target or decide that you need more information.
